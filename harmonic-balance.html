3.2. Harmonic Balance

The tunneling current calculation on the previous page needs the voltage across the junction. In practice, the junction is embedded in an RF circuit (the waveguide probe, tuning circuit, IF circuit, etc.), and the voltage across it depends on how much current it draws, which in turn depends on the voltage. Harmonic balance solves for the junction voltage that satisfies both at the same time.

As before, all values are normalized to the gap voltage, gap current, normal-state resistance and gap frequency.

3.2.1. The embedding circuit

The embedding circuit is linear, so at each frequency it can be reduced to a Thevenin equivalent: a voltage source \(V_{T}\) in series with an impedance \(Z_{T}\). QMix uses one Thevenin circuit for every tone \(f\) and harmonic \(p\) (qmix.circuit.EmbeddingCircuit, attributes vt[f, p] and zt[f, p]). A source can be zero: for example, a harmonic of the LO, or the IF of a mixer, is generated by the junction itself, and it develops a voltage only through the embedding impedance at that frequency.

The DC bias is treated as an ideal voltage source, so the DC voltage across the junction is the bias voltage \(V_0\).

3.2.2. The harmonic balance equations

At each frequency, the junction voltage \(V_{J}\) and current \(I_{J}\) must satisfy Kirchhoff’s voltage law around the Thevenin circuit:

\[V_{J,fp} = V_{T,fp} - Z_{T,fp} \, I_{J,fp}(\mathbf{V}_J),\]

for every tone \(f\) and harmonic \(p\). The current at each frequency is calculated with MTSDA from the voltages at all frequencies, \(\mathbf{V}_J\), so these equations are coupled and strongly non-linear. For \(F\) tones with \(P\) harmonics each, there are \(N = F P\) complex unknowns at every bias voltage. The bias voltages are independent of each other, so QMix solves them all at once, as vectors.

The equations are written as an error function that is zero at the solution:

\[\mathbf{e}(\mathbf{V}_J) = \mathbf{V}_T - \mathbf{Z}_T \, \mathbf{I}_J(\mathbf{V}_J) - \mathbf{V}_J = 0,\]

where the products are taken element by element.

3.2.3. Newton’s method

qmix.harmonic_balance.harmonic_balance() finds the root of \(\mathbf{e}\) with Newton’s method. The first guess treats the junction as a fixed impedance \(Z_J\) (zj_guess, 0.67 by default):

\[\mathbf{V}_J^{(0)} = \mathbf{V}_T \, \frac{Z_J}{Z_J + \mathbf{Z}_T}.\]

Then each iteration linearizes the error around the current estimate and solves for a correction.

The current is not an analytic function of the complex voltage: it depends on the magnitude and phase of each voltage separately, through the Bessel functions. QMix therefore treats the real and imaginary parts of each voltage as separate unknowns. The Jacobian is a real \(2N \times 2N\) matrix, built from \(2 \times 2\) blocks

\[\begin{split}\mathbf{J}_{pq} = \begin{bmatrix} \partial \, \mathrm{Re}\, e_p / \partial \, \mathrm{Re}\, V_q & \partial \, \mathrm{Re}\, e_p / \partial \, \mathrm{Im}\, V_q \\ \partial \, \mathrm{Im}\, e_p / \partial \, \mathrm{Re}\, V_q & \partial \, \mathrm{Im}\, e_p / \partial \, \mathrm{Im}\, V_q \end{bmatrix},\end{split}\]

for every pair of signals \(p, q\). The derivatives are calculated numerically, with steps of \(10^{-3}\) in the real and imaginary part of each voltage. The Newton step is

\[\Delta \mathbf{V}_J = - \mathbf{J}^{-1} \, \mathbf{e}(\mathbf{V}_J).\]

Building the Jacobian takes \(2N\) tunneling current calculations, which is most of the run time. QMix therefore keeps the same Jacobian for further iterations as long as each iteration still at least halves the largest relative error (reuse_jacobian=True, JACOBIAN_REUSE_RATIO). Near the solution, Newton’s method converges so quickly that an outdated Jacobian is usually good enough.

3.2.4. Robustness

When the junction loads the embedding circuit strongly (e.g., a large embedding impedance near the gap voltage), the full Newton step can overshoot and the iteration can diverge. QMix guards against this in three ways (line_search=True, the default):

  • Line search. At each bias voltage, the Newton step is only accepted if it reduces the squared error \(|\mathbf{e}|^2\) enough (the sufficient decrease condition). Otherwise the step is shortened, by backtracking, until it does. The error at the accepted step is also the error for the next iteration, so full steps cost nothing extra.

  • New Jacobian. If no acceptable step exists with an outdated Jacobian, the voltage at that bias point is kept and a new Jacobian is calculated.

  • Restart. If no acceptable step exists even with a new Jacobian (the iteration is stuck near a local minimum of the error rather than a root), the bias point is restarted from the solution at the nearest bias voltage that has already converged.

For multi-tone simulations with one strong tone (e.g., an LO with weak signals), warm_start=True first solves the strongest tone on its own, which is cheap, and uses the result as the starting point for that tone. This often cuts the number of multi-tone iterations by 2-3 times, but not always, so it is not the default.

3.2.5. Convergence

The iteration stops when the relative error, \(|e_{fp}| / |V_{J,fp}|\), is below stop_rerror (\(10^{-3}\) by default) for every signal and bias voltage, or after max_it iterations. Bias voltages that have converged keep being updated while the others are still converging, so most of them end up much more accurate than the tolerance (typically \(\sim 10^{-12}\)). The solution can be checked independently with qmix.harmonic_balance.check_hb_error().

The result is the junction voltage vj[f, p, i] for every tone, harmonic and bias voltage. It can be passed to qmix.qtcurrent.qtcurrent() to calculate the currents at any frequency, e.g., the DC current (the pumped I-V curve) or the current at the IF (the output power of a mixer).

3.2.6. Choosing the parameters

  • num_b: the summation limit for the phase factor coefficients, one value per tone or one for all. It must be larger than the drive level of each tone, \(|V_{J,f1}| / \omega_f\). Tones that are driven hard at a low frequency need many terms, e.g., an IF tone with a large voltage swing. After a first solution, qmix.qtcurrent.suggest_num_b() estimates the limits from the junction voltage. It uses the junction voltage rather than vt, since tones with no source can still have large voltages.

  • num_p (in qmix.circuit.EmbeddingCircuit): the number of harmonics of each tone. Each harmonic adds unknowns and widens the range of the sums. One or two harmonics are usually enough, since the junction capacitance shorts out the higher harmonics.

  • method: how the sums in the tunneling current are evaluated: directly or with the The Fourier-Shift Method. 'auto' picks the Fourier-shift method only when the direct method would need too much memory.
