2. Capabilities and Limitations

2.1. Capabilities

Tunneling currents and harmonic balance

  • Quasiparticle tunneling currents for any number of tones and harmonics (multi-tone spectral domain analysis, qmix.qtcurrent), with a Fourier-shift method that keeps simulations with many tones practical.

  • Harmonic balance with an analytic Jacobian and a line search, to find the junction voltages for a given embedding circuit (qmix.harmonic_balance).

  • Response functions from ideal or model I-V curves, or from measured DC I-V data, including the Kramers-Kronig transform (qmix.respfn).

Mixer analysis

  • Small-signal (Tucker) analysis: conversion gain, shot noise (including its thermal and quantum limits) and noise temperature, at any IF frequency, including an IF of zero (qmix.small_signal).

  • Thermal noise of the embedding circuit (Callen-Welton), and of lossy embedding networks.

Several junctions

  • Twin-junction circuits and series arrays, coupled through a multi-port embedding network (qmix.circuit.MultiJunctionCircuit).

  • Junctions with different areas, gap voltages, normal-state resistances and I-V curves.

  • DC bias networks for series arrays (e.g., with a bias resistor).

Embedding circuits

  • S-parameter networks: read Touchstone files (e.g., from HFSS), connect and terminate networks, and get Thevenin equivalent circuits from them (qmix.network).

  • Circuit elements: transmission lines, microstrip, coplanar waveguide, rectangular waveguide, radial stubs, lumped elements, and ideal components (transformers, hybrids, circulators, attenuators, antennas, de-embedding).

  • Superconducting films: Mattis-Bardeen conductivity and surface impedance, including the kinetic inductance and loss of superconducting microstrip (qmix.superconductor).

Experimental data

  • Analysis of measured DC and pumped I-V curves and IF spectra: recover the embedding impedance and voltage, and the gain and noise temperature (Y-factor, shot-noise calibration) (qmix.exp).

Many of these results are checked against SuperMix, an independent C++ library for SIS mixers (see QMix vs SuperMix below).

2.2. Limitations

Physics

  • Only the quasiparticle tunneling current is simulated: there is no Josephson (pair) current, so no Shapiro steps, Josephson noise or magnetic field.

  • The response function comes from one DC I-V curve, so heating and changes of the gap with LO power or temperature are not included.

  • Noise is only calculated by the small-signal analysis, which assumes a single LO (with harmonics) and weak signals. Multi-tone simulations give currents and gain, but not noise (e.g., under saturation or with a strong second tone).

  • Harmonic balance finds one solution per bias point, so hysteresis in series arrays (e.g., up/down bias sweeps) cannot be followed.

Embedding circuits

  • The thermal noise of a lossy network is calculated at one temperature; elements don’t have their own temperatures.

  • There are no active devices (e.g., IF amplifiers): the IF chain is described by its noise temperature only.

  • The coplanar waveguide model is lossless (no conductor loss or kinetic inductance), and there is no stripline model.

Numerics

  • The cost of the tunneling current grows quickly with the number of tones and with strong pumping of low-frequency tones (many Bessel terms). The Fourier-shift method helps, but it is less accurate (~1e-7 to 1e-9).

  • Frequencies are matched after rounding, so tones closer than ~1e-4 of the gap frequency are treated as the same frequency.

  • Harmonic balance can converge slowly for very strong pumping, sharp gap knees and series arrays.

With the same inputs, the two agree to round-off or close to it: tunneling currents to ~1e-15, harmonic balance to ~1e-9, small-signal admittance and noise matrices to ~1e-14 (with SuperMix’s physical constants, which are older CODATA values), passive circuit elements to ~1e-14, and normal-metal lines to round-off. For superconducting films, the agreement is set by SuperMix’s numerical integration (~1e-8) and its table of the energy gap vs temperature (~1e-4 above T/Tc = 0.04; see Superconducting Transmission Lines).
