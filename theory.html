3. Theory

This section describes the methods that QMix uses to simulate SIS junctions. A simulation has two parts, and a mixer simulation can add a third:

  1. Multi-tone spectral domain analysis (MTSDA) gives the quasiparticle tunneling current through the junction for a known voltage across it, with any number of tones and harmonics. This is what qmix.qtcurrent.qtcurrent() calculates.

  2. Harmonic balance finds the voltage across the junction when the junction is embedded in a circuit, i.e., the voltage that is consistent with both the tunneling current and the embedding circuit. This is what qmix.harmonic_balance.harmonic_balance() calculates. It calls the tunneling current calculation many times.

  3. Small-signal analysis gives the conversion gain and noise of a mixer from the LO state alone, when the RF and IF signals are weak (Tucker theory), for one junction or several junctions in one network. This is what qmix.small_signal calculates.

The embedding circuit can be built from superconducting transmission lines: the next page describes the models for the films (Mattis-Bardeen theory) and for microstrip lines in qmix.superconductor and qmix.network. The last page describes the Fourier-shift method, an alternative way to evaluate the sums in MTSDA that makes simulations with many tones practical.

The methods follow Kittara’s thesis (2002), Withington, Kittara and Yassin (2003), and Kittara, Withington and Yassin (2007); see References. Equation numbers in the text and in the QMix source code (e.g., “Eqn. 5.25”) refer to Chapter 5 of Kittara’s thesis.
